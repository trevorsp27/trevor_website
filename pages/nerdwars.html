    Game

    NerdWars

    A platform fighter where the whole roster is my high school friend group. Kel drew every character as a 16×16 sprite back in 2018, we planned a Smash Bros. clone around them, and then we never built it. The sprites sat in a folder for seven years. This is the game they were for.

    Play

    Two players, one keyboard.

    CLICK TO PLAY the game takes the keyboard while you're in it

    Click the screen to hand it the keyboard; click anywhere else on the page to give it back. Fullscreen is worth it — the game draws at 320×180 and scales by whole numbers, and every 16:9 screen is an exact multiple of that, so it fills the display with no blur and no black bars.

    Play online

    One of you creates a room and reads the code out; the other types it in. The connection is browser to browser — there is no server in the middle, and nothing to sign up for.

    Both machines run the same simulation and swap only button presses, so what you see is what your opponent sees. That costs a few frames of input delay: fine between nearby friends, heavy across an ocean. If the two ever disagree the match stops rather than quietly showing you different fights.

    Controls

      Move Jump Drop Attack Special Shield Ult
    Player 1 AD W S F G H R
    Player 2 ←→ ↑ ↓ , . / ;
    • Damage percent does not kill you. It decides how far the next hit throws you. Get knocked off the screen and you lose a stock.
    • You get two air jumps. Hold down while airborne to fall faster, or down plus jump to drop through a platform.
    • Shield plus a direction rolls, and you are invincible in the middle of it. Shield plus down is a spot dodge.
    • The bar under your stocks is your ult. It fills as damage is dealt and taken — faster when you are the one being beaten on — and flashes white when it is ready.
    • Playing alone is fine — pick 1 Player and the second fighter is a CPU.

    The roster

    Three of the six movesets are not inventions — the art for them already existed, and the game just uses it.

    • Kel is the only character with a full drawn attack animation, plus a four-frame spinning bone. He drew himself a projectile, so he plays as a zoner.
    • Reese is the only one with a complete second sprite set: a full shirtless walk cycle. In game that is a transformation — faster and stronger, but he flies further when hit.
    • AutisNick throws pizza — three drawn slices, and the crust trails the direction of travel, so they are not mirrors of each other. Hold down to drop one through the floor, or up to lob a rainbow over a platform, which is the one place the flat shot cannot reach.
    • Trev swings a flaming laser sword on the way up, which doubles as his recovery.
    • JohnnyHam and Ladeane only ever got movement art, so their specials are placeholders picked to cover distinct playstyles rather than personalities. Several ults are placeholders too.

    Five stages, all built from tilesets found in the same 2018 folder. Which tiles survived decided what each stage could be: two of them have a drawn ceiling, so those are enclosed arenas you cannot be knocked out of the top of. Lava only ever got a floor tile, so on that stage the floor is the hazard rather than the ground.
